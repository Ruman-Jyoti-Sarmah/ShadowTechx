AI, only where it saves someone real time.

We don't add AI because it's on a slide. We add it when a specific person spends a specific hour every day on something a model can do in seconds — and someone still checks it.

How we approach it

Start with the task, not the model.

Before picking a provider, we work out what a good answer even looks like, what it does when it's wrong, and where a person has to sign off. If nobody catches mistakes, the feature isn't ready.

We also build the boring safeguards: what data gets sent, what gets logged, what happens when the API goes down or the bill spikes. Most of the failures in AI features are these, not the model itself.

Where it usually helps

  • Search across your own documents and past emails.
  • Drafting routine replies a person then edits.
  • Sorting and tagging incoming enquiries.
  • Summarising long forms, reports or tickets.
  • Answering staff questions from an internal handbook.

Before you commit

Some of them don't need AI.

Plenty of tasks that look like an AI problem are really a search box, a better form, or a filter. That's cheaper, faster and nobody has to trust a model.
